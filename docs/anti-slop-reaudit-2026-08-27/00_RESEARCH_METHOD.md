# Research method

Date: **2026-08-27**  
Scope: Mathos WebMCP submission, landing route and complete `/learn` interaction.

## Question

Does the product express a Mathos-specific learning and browser-agent mechanism, or does it merely exchange one fashionable generated-UI bundle for another?

“AI slop” is treated here as an operational design-risk label, not an authorship detector or a validated academic construct. A visual trait is not a defect by itself. It becomes evidence of an unmade decision when a cluster appears without a reason grounded in mathematics, learner agency, product state, accessibility, or Mathos identity.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Current rendered pixels and behavior in the target browser.
2. Current repository source, tests, and state/tool contracts.
3. Primary standards, product documentation, and current upstream source.
4. Peer-reviewed or primary academic research.
5. Reproducible empirical datasets and scanners, with their methods audited.
6. Practitioner catalogues and detectors.
7. Community discourse and the supplied Instagram Reel as hypothesis generators only.

No aesthetic claim is promoted merely because several blogs repeat it. No single screenshot is used to infer AI authorship. Clusters, mechanisms, false positives, and confidence are recorded explicitly.

## Procedure

### 1. Repository and product observation

- Inspected the current branch and source rather than inheriting the 2026-08-26 audit verdict.
- Ran the production preview at `http://127.0.0.1:4322`.
- Exercised all six registered WebMCP tools in Chrome 151 with `WebMCPTesting`.
- Captured current connected, unavailable, empty, populated, broken, refusal, annotation, proposal, practice-complete, transfer, and immediate-transfer-evidence states.
- Captured 1440, 1280, 1024, 768, 390, 125%, 150%, 200%, and reduced-motion variants.
- Inspected screenshots as rendered images, not just DOM/source.
- Searched source for current shadcn, Tailwind, font, color, radius, motion, copy, and second-order anti-slop fingerprints.

The immutable baseline is in [`before/`](./before/).

### 2. Seed inspection

The supplied Instagram Reel was opened in a signed-in interactive browser and watched through its full list. Screenshots and sampled frames are preserved in [`instagram-seed/`](./instagram-seed/). Its 30 claims were recorded, including gradients, Lucide, white backgrounds, shadows, three-card rows, emojis, glass, em dashes, fashionable fonts, side stripes, testimonials, bento grids, terminal windows, slogan templates, checkmark bullets, pricing tiers, missing demos, rounded corners, purple/black, missing loaders, orbs, dot grids, sparkles, animated arrows, missing legal pages, hover animation, neon, and pastel palettes.

The Reel is deliberately not treated as validation. Its examples are tiny, its method is unpublished, and its own comments contest the list as overbroad. It is a useful taste signal and vocabulary source.

### 3. Parallel evidence streams

Three independent research roles were used without sharing conclusions during collection:

- academic/HCI evidence and learning-transfer research;
- official standards, WebMCP, Anthropic, Vercel, shadcn, and Tailwind sources;
- empirical/community/detector and product-reference evidence.

The main audit independently reopened the highest-consequence sources and verified the current rendered and source-level Mathos claims.

### 4. Direction tournament

Four directions vary information architecture, spatial organization, interaction emphasis, material metaphor, density, motion, and the relationship between learner work and machine evidence. They are scored on the 13 criteria required by the brief, five points each. Scores are comparative design judgments, not measurements; the reasoning and risks matter more than the arithmetic.

### 5. Implementation and re-audit protocol

After direction approval:

1. freeze the canonical design contract;
2. implement without weakening the reducer, deterministic math engine, persistence, tool policy, proposal consent, transfer lock, or source attribution;
3. capture the complete after matrix from a clean browser state;
4. run five isolated adversarial reviews;
5. reconcile detector output with human visual judgment;
6. run unit tests, typecheck, production build, real WebMCP journeys, keyboard, reflow, zoom, reduced-motion, contrast, state-announcement, and mathematical-hostility checks;
7. report residual high and medium risks without converting “not observed” into “zero.”

## Falsifiability rules

- If a trait is common but clearly serves a Mathos-specific task, it is not removed to satisfy a detector.
- If a detector score improves while comprehension, accessibility, or product identity worsens, the detector loses.
- If an element cannot name the state, decision, or comprehension job it performs, it is presumed ornamental.
- If source and pixels disagree, pixels win for visual judgment; source wins for hidden semantics and behavior.
- If current evidence is indirect or methodologically weak, confidence is lowered instead of laundering it through precise language.

## Known limitations

- No authorship-labelled, representative 2026 web-UI population benchmark exists in the reviewed evidence.
- The strongest broad visual lists are lexical community data or self-authored detector catalogues.
- The academic homogenization paper is a sociotechnical risk framework, not a visual-prevalence experiment.
- Mathos usability was not tested with recruited learners or assistive-technology users during this re-audit.
- Local browser performance is useful regression evidence, not production-network evidence.
- Direction scores and the boundary between intentional style and counter-fashion remain partly subjective.
