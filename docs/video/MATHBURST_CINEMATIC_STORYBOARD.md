## The film in one sentence

A subtle error inside the Gamma recurrence opens into a living mathematical universe: an
integral becomes probability, probability trains a tiny transformer, attention becomes
barycentric geometry, a triangle lifts into a 3D simplex, and its lattice resolves into a
Ramanujan partition pattern—all while real WebMCP calls prove the tutor inhabits the same
world as the learner.

## Cinematic thesis

This is one continuous mathematical argument, not a reel of unrelated impressive topics.
Every cut is earned by a true structural equivalence already visible on the canvas:

`area → probability mass → softmax weights → barycentric weights → simplex coordinates →
integer lattice → generating-function coefficients`.

The learner is graphite. The tutor is purple. Canonical mathematics is crisp LaTeX;
handwriting carries intent, uncertainty, and correction. WebMCP calls appear as restrained,
real evidence attached to visible consequences—never as a terminal, fake chat, or detached
developer overlay.

The animation language may have the clarity and transformation-first energy associated with
excellent mathematical explainers: dark focus fields when depth is required, luminous
geometry, sparse color, and motion that explains an invariant. It must retain Mathburst's
own ivory/graphite/purple identity and never imitate another creator's exact visual package.

## Shot plan

### 0:00–0:14 — Cold open: the hidden sign error

**Opening frame.** Macro view of ivory graph paper. The learner has written:

`Γ(9/2) = ∫₀∞ x⁷ᐟ²e⁻ˣ dx = [−x⁷ᐟ²e⁻ˣ]₀∞ − (7/2)Γ(7/2)`

The prompt reads: “Don’t finish it. Mark the exact place my reasoning breaks.” No title card
or detached app chrome appears for the first two seconds.

**Action.** A purple tutor cursor enters. A tiny real trace reads
`get_selection → get_objects → create_objects`. The tutor circles the minus before the
recurrence term and writes: `v = −e⁻ˣ. Two negatives.` The learner replaces it with a plus and
unfolds `= (7/2)(5/2)(3/2)(1/2)√π = 105√π/16` as a separate graphite commit. The original
mistake remains undoable.

**Camera.** Begin at roughly 165–180% product scale, centered on the incorrect recurrence.
Drift with the learner's final stroke, then rack attention through opacity toward the purple
annotation. Pull back at 0:11 to expose Mathburst, Tutor attribution, and one shared-history
entry.

**WebMCP proof.** The circle, strike, and note are a real `create_objects` commit. The
learner correction is a separate human commit in the same reducer/history. Undo can reverse
either action.

**Match transition.** The circled minus stretches vertically and becomes the integral sign;
the purple underline becomes its lower bound.

### 0:14–0:29 — Ink becomes semantic mathematics

**Frame.** The handwritten recurrence continues into a photographed derivation of
`Γ(α + 1) = ∫₀∞ xᵅe⁻ˣ dx = αΓ(α)`.

**Action.** `reconstruct_problem` lifts the source into editable LaTeX. Brief purple
uncertainty brackets distinguish `s` from `5` and confirm the infinite bound. Then
`audit_reconstruction` checks the recurrence and a human approval commits the clean
conversion. Source and semantic equation remain linked rather than replacing one another.

**Camera.** A 420 ms lateral track, then a 300 ms settle with the recurrence occupying at
least one third of frame height.

**WebMCP proof.** Propose, audit, and approve are three honest visible states. Reconstruction
objects join their frame's semantic children.

**Match transition.** The recurrence baseline extends into an x-axis; the superscript on
`x²` drops onto the curve's peak.

### 0:29–0:49 — Repeated integration by parts becomes probability

**Frame.** The live graph of `g(x) = x⁷ᐟ²e⁻ˣ` grows left-to-right. Its total shaded area is
`Γ(9/2) = 105√π/16`. A normalization step transforms it into
`p(x) = x⁷ᐟ²e⁻ˣ / Γ(9/2)`, now with total area `1`.

**Action.** `graph_expression` links equation and graph. The learner drags an upper bound
`b`; the CDF region `P(X ≤ b)` and numeric area update continuously. A tangent follows the
bound. The tutor changes the exponent parameter `α`; equation, normalization, curve, mode,
tangent, and area respond as one semantic state. The final probability bin explicitly owns
the infinite tail so the displayed bin weights sum to exactly one.

**Camera.** Dolly from recurrence to shaded region. Hold still during parameter change so
motion belongs to the mathematics.

**WebMCP proof.** `graph_expression` creates the representation; later `update_objects`
changes the parameter. Each call resolves into a visible target highlight and attributed
history row.

**Match transition.** The normalized shaded area separates into three vertical probability
bins `wⱼ = ∫Bⱼp(x)dx`. The film explicitly calls these a bridge between normalized weights
and attention—not the same mechanism. The bins rise into logits, then softmax computes a new
distribution.

### 0:49–1:18 — Train a tiny transformer, for real

**Frame.** The three probability bins become token probabilities beside the prompt
`the area equals [MASK]`. Token embeddings unfold onto a coordinate plane. Native canvas
matrices `W_Q`, `W_K`, and `W_V` project query, key, and value vectors.

**Action A — attention geometry.** Dot products appear on angle arcs. Scaled logits pass
through softmax; ribbon widths equal the displayed weights. Editing one matrix entry moves
the vectors, angles, logits, and ribbons together.

**Action B — one honest training step.** The prediction distribution, correct target, and
cross-entropy `L = −log p(target)` appear. The learner presses `TRAIN 1 STEP`. A real,
deterministic gradient update changes the tiny model weights; the correct-token probability
rises and loss falls. A short loss sparkline records step 0 and step 1. The UI explicitly
calls this a “tiny transformer training step,” never “training a frontier LLM.”

**Tutor turn.** `inspect_math` reads the current matrices and loss; `update_objects` performs
a second reversible Tutor-authored step. No hidden chain-of-thought is shown.

**Camera.** Start at the editable matrix, slide along `Q`, settle on the angle wedge, then
track the thickening correct-token ribbon to the falling loss. Every animation reaches a
rest state.

**Match transition.** Freeze the three strongest attention ribbons. Their endpoints become
triangle vertices `A, B, C`; their softmax weights relabel as `α, β, γ`.

### 1:18–1:46 — Attention weights become Olympiad geometry

**Frame.** The exact identity is written once:
`P = αA + βB + γC`, with `α + β + γ = 1`. The attention-weighted sum is now visibly the
barycentric point of triangle `ABC`.

**Action A — barycentrics.** Dragging `P` updates three signed subtriangle areas and the
coordinate triple. The tutor moves `P` to `[1:1:1]`; it lands at the centroid and remains
undoable.

**Action B — homothety and spiral similarity.** The triangle expands into two tangent
circles and homothety center `O`. Dragging `A` recomputes `A′, B′, C′`, the circles, and
`OA′/OA = OB′/OB`. `construct_geometry` adds the spiral-similarity center, equal-angle arcs,
rotation arc, and scale ray. The source point moves once more to prove the construction is
semantic, not flattened artwork.

**Camera.** Follow dependencies, not cursors. Use one shallow orbit-like 2D track around the
spiral center while preserving undistorted product capture.

**Match transition.** A fourth attention weight `δ` appears. The triangle's area regions
fold upward; `α + β + γ + δ = 1` becomes a tetrahedral probability simplex.

### 1:46–2:03 — The probability simplex becomes 3D geometry

**Frame.** A restrained perspective tetrahedron rotates on the canvas. Four vertices are
token outcomes; an interior point carries barycentric/simplex coordinates
`[α:β:γ:δ]`. Face projections and one slicing plane make the 3D relation readable.

**Action.** Dragging one weight moves the interior point while preserving the normalized
sum. A section plane sweeps the tetrahedron; the 2D triangle from the previous act appears
as a literal cross-section. `visualize_concept` creates the simplex and `update_objects`
moves the semantic weights.

**Truth boundary.** This is a mathematically projected 3D object, not a physics engine. The
projection, normalized coordinates, and section relation must be correct for the visible
state.

**Match transition.** The tetrahedron pauses on a lattice subdivision. Its integer lattice
points detach, flatten, and reorder by an integer index `n`.

### 2:03–2:23 — Integer lattice to Ramanujan

**Frame.** Lattice spikes receive Gaussian smoothing and become a theta-like curve. The
points then reorganize into Ferrers diagrams and the partition generating function:

`P(q) = ∏ₘ₌₁∞ (1 − qᵐ)⁻¹ = Σₙ₌₀∞ p(n)qⁿ`.

**Action.** Expanding finite factors creates coefficients as real computed objects. The
coefficients stream into five residue lanes; the `5n + 4` lane resolves to multiples of
five, revealing Ramanujan's congruence `p(5n + 4) ≡ 0 (mod 5)`. The product labels this as a
visualized theorem, not a complete proof. A compact finite recurrence is checked by induction
only if its displayed base case and step are actually implemented; otherwise induction is
omitted rather than faked.

**Visual language.** Dark focus field, luminous curve, ivory equations, graphite lattice,
and a single purple invariant. No rainbow particle soup.

**WebMCP proof.** `inspect_math` reads the generating function; `visualize_concept` creates
the lattice/partition representation; the resulting objects remain selectable and undoable.

**Match transition.** The five residue lanes compress into five tool-family lanes.

### 2:23–2:34 — WebMCP crescendo

**Frame.** A rapid traversal of the same world shows representative real calls from all

## Cinematography rules

- The first collaboration occurs by 0:05; the product thesis is clear by 0:14.
- No title card, slide, terminal, fake chat, stock footage, detached feature card, or
  video-only mathematical mutation.
- Every transition preserves a real mathematical object or invariant. A topic change without
  structural continuity is cut.
- Human actions are graphite; Tutor actions and temporary focus are purple.
- Handwriting is for mistakes, marks, arrows, and short questions. Truth-bearing equations
  and computed values remain crisp LaTeX.
- Every animation has one explanatory job and a deterministic rest state. No ambient loops,
  glowing dashboard noise, particle fields, or decorative 3D orbiting.
- Important labels stay inside a 7% title-safe margin and survive 1280×720 downscaling.
- The number-theory act may reveal and verify a theorem. It must not claim a complete proof
  of Ramanujan's congruence unless the product genuinely contains one.
